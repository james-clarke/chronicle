Know what you actually worked on.

Chronicle watches which window has focus on your machine. A small model running on the same machine turns that into a timeline of tasks, a standup draft each morning, and a weekly report. Nothing leaves your computer.

A note on what you're looking at. Every screenshot on this page is my own machine, taken today, unedited. Those are my real tasks, my real Jira keys, my real repos. I've used Chronicle every working day for the last few weeks and it's already the thing I open first in the morning. It is early and rough in places. I'm putting it in front of people now because I want to know whether it would be useful to you, and what it would need to be.

A day, as it happened.

Focus events get grouped into blocks. Each block is matched to a task you declared, or to one Chronicle inferred from window titles, branch names and Jira keys. Every block is correctable: reassign it, split it, rename it.

The band at the top is the shape of the day: focused, away, switching. That number at the top left is time actually spent on something, not hours the laptop was open.

The week, without a spreadsheet.

Longest block, deep-work total, context switches, the hour that fragments most, how this week compares to last. Tasks roll up by project with real durations. A one-click summary drafts the narrative for you.

Ask your own history.

"What did I work on this morning?" "How long did the merge take?" The answer comes from the model on your machine, reading your local database. No API key, no account, nothing uploaded.

It is a 1.7B-parameter model on a CPU. Answers take a while and are sometimes wrong. They are also grounded in what actually happened, which turns out to matter more.

It knows your repos and your tickets.

Point it at the git repositories you work in and it picks up commits, checkouts and branch names as they happen. Connect Jira over MCP and a ticket key in a window title becomes a task with its real title and context attached.

Connections are the only thing that ever talks to a network, and only to the servers you configured.

How it works

  1. Capture. A tiny daemon records which window has focus and when you go idle. That is the whole input.
  2. Derive. A local model (Qwen3 1.7B via llama.cpp) groups focus events into tasks and writes one-line descriptions.
  3. Correct. You fix what it got wrong in the timeline. The corrections feed the next derivation.
  4. Use. Standup draft in the morning, report on Friday, chat whenever.

Where your data lives

One SQLite file in your home directory. The model runs in-process. There is no account, no sync, no telemetry, no crash reporting, and no analytics on this page either. If you want it gone, delete one folder.
