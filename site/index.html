Your day, your work, as it happened.

Focus events get grouped into blocks. Each block is matched to a task you declared, or to one Chronicle inferred.

Every task, one card.

Open a block for its description, every session that fed it, and where the time went. Chat about it, rename it, close it.

The week, without a spreadsheet.

Longest block, deep-work total, context switches, the hour that fragments most, how this week compares to last.

Ask your own history.

"What did I work on this morning?" Answered by a model on your machine, from your local database.

It knows your repos and your tickets.

Point it at your git repositories and connect Jira over MCP. Commits, branches and ticket keys become task context.

How it works

  1. Capture. A small daemon records which window has focus and when you go idle.
  2. Derive. A local model groups focus events into tasks and describes them.
  3. Correct. Fix what it got wrong. Corrections feed the next derivation.
  4. Use. Standup draft in the morning, report on Friday, chat whenever.

Where your data lives

One SQLite file in your home directory. The model runs in-process. No account, no sync, no telemetry.
